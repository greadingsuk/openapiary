## 1. Confirmed configuration

| Item | Value | How confirmed |
|---|---|---|
| Board | HX711 module, `E+ E- A+ A-` on one side, `DT SCK VCC GND` on the other | — |
| Channel / gain | A, gain 128 | library default, 25 clock pulses |
| VCC | **3.306 V** (3V3 pad, not VBUS) | measured |
| Sample rate | **10 SPS** (RATE pin to GND) | `dump` reports 81.9–82.1 ms/sample |
| Settle delay | `delay(400)` after power-up | correct for 10 SPS — **do not shorten** |
| MCU pins | `PIN_HX711_DT = D2` (P0.04), `PIN_HX711_SCK = D3` (P0.05) | source |
| Library | `bogde/HX711@^0.7.5` | `platformio.ini` |

**The HX711 is ratiometric.** Excitation voltage cancels out — it does **not** affect
counts/kg. Raising excitation does not improve resolution.

## 2. Counts per kilogram

Full-scale differential input at gain 128 ≈ **±(AVDD/128)/2 ≈ ±12.9 mV** at 3.3 V.
Over ±8,388,608 counts that is roughly **650,000 counts per mV**.

For four summing 50 kg cells (200 kg full scale):

| Cell spec | Output at FS | Predicted counts/kg |
|---|---|---|
| 1.0 mV/V | 3.3 mV | ~10,700 |
| 2.0 mV/V | 6.6 mV | ~21,400 |

Measured in practice:

| Rig | counts/kg | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| Open Apiary | **13,953** | 0.07 g/count |
| smart-hive-scale | **26,913** | 0.04 g/count |

Both ~28% above prediction — consistent systematic offset, so the model is sound and the
difference between rigs is the **cell sensitivity variant**, not a fault. See
[load-cells.md §2](load-cells.md).

For a 40 kg hive this is massively over-resolved either way. Resolution is not a
limiting factor on this project.

## 3. Measured noise floor

Bench, static load, `-e xiaoble` production build:

| Condition | Within-burst spread (counts) | Approx grams |
|---|---|---|
| Hardened read path, radio off | 159–165 | ~26 g |
| Production path, radio off | 165 | ~26 g |
| Production path, advertising @20 ms | 151 | ~24 g |
| Production path, **live GATT connection** | **138** | ~22 g |

**Radio activity does not corrupt the bit-bang read.** Not advertising, not a live
connection. The connected case measured *quietest*. This was the leading hypothesis at
the start of the investigation and it is refuted.

After calibration, settled readings on the sugar bag gave **5 g peak-to-peak, spread
4–12 g**. The ADC and sensor chain are not the problem.

## 3a. Excitation duty cycle — THE BENCH TRAP

**Sampling fast makes the scale drift. The diagnostic tool can manufacture the very
instability you are trying to diagnose.** This cost hours on 2026-09-07.

Continuous bridge excitation self-heats the strain gauges. These cells shift roughly
**100 g per °C**, so a couple of degrees of self-heating is hundreds of grams of apparent
weight change.

Measured, same rig, static, nothing touched:

| Interval | HX711 powered | Duty | Excursion over 15 min |
|---|---|---|---|
| `monprod 5` | ~1.4 s per 5 s | 28% | **480 g**, oscillating ±250 g |
| `monprod 60` | ~1.4 s per 60 s | 2.3% | **89 g**, and only 21 g over the last 8 min |

Each individual sample stayed tight (4–15 g spread) in both cases. It is the **zero** that
walks, not the noise.

### Production is unaffected

| Firmware | Cadence | Duty |
|---|---|---|
| nRF52 `main.cpp` `SUMMER_MEAS_MS` | one read / 15 min | **0.15%** |
| ESP32 `main_v2.cpp` `SLEEP_DURATION_US` | one read / 15 min | **0.2%** |

Both power the HX711 down between reads. Neither ever sees this.

> This also explains why the ESP32 rig appeared inherently more stable — it deep sleeps,
> so it can never be polled fast enough to show the effect. Same cells, same physics.

### Rules

- **Never bench-measure absolute level at intervals under 30 s.** Use `monprod 30` or
  slower for anything where the reading's *level* matters.
- Fast intervals (`monprod 2`) are fine for **spread** and for **step** measurements taken
  within a few seconds of each other.
- Corner sweeps: use 30–60 s intervals and reference each corner to bracketing empty
  readings so residual drift cancels.
- **Do not shorten the 15-minute production cadence** without re-testing this.

## 4. Firmware read paths — known defect

There are **two** different HX711 read paths in `firmware/src/hx711_helper.h`:

| Path | Used by | Protection |
|---|---|---|
| `hx711_raw_read_timeout()` | `tare`, `cal`, `raw`, `mon`, `monble`, `show`, `dump` | `noInterrupts()` around each SCK-high window, 500 ms DOUT-ready timeout, correct 25th pulse |
| `hx711_read_median()` → `g_hx.get_units(1)` | **production measure cycle**, GATT `OA_CHAR_SAMPLE`, `monprod` | **none** — bogde's critical-section guards are ESP32/AVR/ESP8266 only; on nRF52 there is no critical section and no timeout |

**Consequences:**

1. `tare` and `cal` derive their numbers from one path; production measures on the other.
   Any systematic difference between them becomes a calibration bias.
2. Observed: `cal 0.996` was accepted, then the measurement path immediately read
   **0.968** — a **~2.8%** offset. Consistent with this defect. Not conclusively
   attributed (settling is an alternative explanation), but it is the leading candidate.
3. `hx711_read_median()` additionally lacks: ready-gate, discard reads after wake,
   saturation guard, `pinMode` re-init.

**This is a code-quality defect worth fixing, but measurement showed it is NOT the cause
of the instability that started the investigation.**

### Reference implementation

`smart-hive-scale/firmware/src/main_v2.cpp` `readWeight()` is the proven pattern:

- `wait_ready_timeout(2000)` before the first read
- 3 discard reads @ 50 ms, each ready-gated
- 10 samples, **trimmed mean** (drop 2 low + 2 high, average the middle 6)
- saturation guard: reject `|raw| >= 8388600`
- `scale.begin()` re-init per read
- radio fully off during the read
- returns 0.0 + logs on failure rather than propagating garbage

Median-of-10 has ~1.25× the standard deviation of a mean-of-10 for Gaussian noise, so
the current estimator is strictly noisier than the proven one even when every sample is
valid. Offline replay on `dump` fixtures scored trimmed-mean-1/1 at **56%** of median's
SD.

### Side by side

| | ESP32 `readWeight()` | nRF52 `hx711_read_median()` |
|---|---|---|
| Ready gate | `wait_ready_timeout(2000)` | none |
| Discard reads after wake | **3, ready-gated, 50 ms apart** | **none** |
| Settle | via discards | `delay(400)` then read immediately |
| Estimator | trimmed mean of 10 | median of 10 |
| Re-init per read | `scale.begin()` | none |
| Critical section | yes (ESP32 path in bogde) | **no** (nRF52 not covered) |

`delay(400)` sits exactly on the HX711's first-valid-output boundary at 10 SPS, and we
then read with no discards. That is a genuine defect worth fixing.

### But it does not explain the mechanical drift

Tested 2026-09-07 on the bench harness:

| Condition | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| Static, undisturbed, 43 samples over 86 s | **8 g p-p — flat** |
| Immediately after a 3.85 kg load change | **~850 g over 30 s, still moving** |

Same firmware, same code path, same sample rate. A settling or thermal defect in the read
path would drift in both cases; this only drifts after mechanical disturbance. **Firmware
is not the cause of the creep or the corner-load error** — see
[load-cells.md](load-cells.md) §2 and §6a for what actually was.

Fix the read path anyway (item 1 below), then re-run the comparison so the variable is
removed entirely rather than argued about.

## 5. Calibration CLI reference

USB power puts the firmware into calibration mode automatically (115200 baud).

| Command | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `tare` | store current raw reading as zero offset |
| `cal <kg>` | compute scale factor from the mass currently on the platform |
| `save` | persist cal/tare to `/cal.txt` (auto-runs after tare/cal) |
| `show` | stored cal/tare/packetId + live raw reading |
| `raw [n]` | average of n raw samples with min/max/spread |
| `dump [n] [prod]` | every individual raw count as CSV; `prod` uses the unprotected path. Preamble reports ms/sample → reveals RATE strapping |
| `mon [int_s] [dur_s]` | stream raw/net/kg/temp/spread/batt as CSV (hardened path) |
| `monprod [int_s] [dur_s]` | stream the **production** estimator as a readable table with automatic segment summaries. **Use `int_s` ≥ 30 for level measurements — see §3a** |
| `monble [int_s] [dur_s]` | like `mon` but with the radio advertising/connected |
| `radio on\|off` | leave aggressive 20 ms advertising running across other commands |
| `awake [s]` | hold the HX711 powered so excitation can be probed (it sleeps between reads, which collapses AVDD and mimics a dead bridge) |
| `logs`, `cfg`, `diagtest [n]`, `batcal <V>`, `ble [s]`, `reboot`, `exit` | misc |

### `monprod` output

```
  time     weight    spread  state    temp
  -------------------------------------------
  00:01    -0.014 kg    10 g  steady   23.5
  ...
  ------------------------------------------------------------
  SEGMENT   n=7   mean= -0.005 kg   p-p=  18 g   (baseline)
  ------------------------------------------------------------
```

A **segment** closes when the load changes, and prints count, mean, peak-to-peak and the
**step against the previous segment**. That step is the number the corner-load and
calibration tests want — read it straight off the screen.

Segments close on **either** a spread spike **or** a 0.3 kg shift from the running mean.
Spread alone is insufficient — a load removal was once observed with a spread of only
22 g. Runs shorter than 3 samples are discarded so a wobble mid-handling cannot emit a
false step.

**`state` column:** `steady` vs `MOVING`. **Never take a reading while `spread_g` is
elevated** — high spread means the operator is still handling the load. This rule was
broken once during testing and produced a wrong conclusion.

Sending any line stops a running `monprod`. Sending a *new* `monprod` while one is
running only stops the old one — you must send the command twice.

## 6. Build and flash

```powershell
cd "openapiary/firmware"
& "$env:USERPROFILE\.platformio\penv\Scripts\pio.exe" run -e xiaoble
& "$env:USERPROFILE\.platformio\penv\Scripts\pio.exe" run -e xiaoble -t upload
```

Close the serial monitor first — upload needs COM5 free.

**Always capture baselines on `-e xiaoble` (production).** The `xiaoble_diag` env sets
`CFG_LOGGER=1 / CFG_DEBUG=2`, which changes interrupt and timing behaviour and would
invalidate the comparison.

Monitor:

```powershell
& "$env:USERPROFILE\.platformio\penv\Scripts\pio.exe" device monitor -e xiaoble --echo
```

Tare and calibration survive a reflash (stored in `/cal.txt`).

## 7. Outstanding fix list

| # | Item | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Unify the read paths — route everything through `hx711_raw_read_timeout()`, add ready-gate, 3 discard reads, saturation guard, return NAN + quality flag on failure, retire the duplicate helpers | High — causes calibration bias |
| 2 | Replace median with trimmed mean (evidence in §4) | Medium |
| 3 | Replace the 5 kg friction guard with a measured spread threshold | Medium |
| 4 | **BTHome negative-weight clamp** — `bthome.h` encodes weight as `uint16` constrained `0..65535`, so any negative reading displays as 0.00 kg in the app | Medium |
| 5 | **Battery ADC is bimodal** — `readBatteryVoltageCal()` alternates ~3.82 V / ~4.22 V on consecutive reads. Reported voltage is not trustworthy | Medium |
| 6 | Overnight drift characterisation + temperature regression | Low |

## 8. MCU suitability (nRF52840 vs ESP32)

**Precision is not MCU-dependent.** The HX711 does the measurement; the MCU only clocks
out 24 bits. Precision comes from the ADC, the cells, the excitation and the mechanics —
all shared with the ESP32 project. Measured performance on the nRF52840 (5 g p-p) is
good by any standard.

**Power strongly favours the nRF52840**: BLE TX is single-digit mA against the ESP32's
100–250 mA WiFi bursts, and deep sleep is ~1–2 µA. That is why the chip was chosen.

**Unverified:** actual current draw and battery endurance have never been measured. The
power *architecture* is favourable; the battery life figure does not exist yet. Fix list
item 5 also means reported battery voltage cannot currently be trusted.

**One nRF52-specific caveat:** the bogde library's interrupt-safe critical section is
applied only on ESP32/AVR/ESP8266, so the production read path runs unprotected here.
Measured as harmless, but see fix list item 1.
